\section{Choosing a step for output-only recovery}
\label{sec:choice}
The sign test changes the choice of step when unique recovery from one saved state is a requirement across exact priors and scales. At ratio $1/16$, one two-call DPM++2S step meets that requirement, whereas a fixed Heun schedule needs at least four substeps and eight denoiser calls. A learned checkpoint map $\widehat F$ inherits injectivity from the exact map when $\operatorname{Lip}(\widehat F-F)<c_*$, with $c_*$ the lower bound in \cref{eq:colip}.

That condition is rarely checkable for a trained network, so in practice we recommend screening candidate steps for source recovery on paired calibration states, at the intended interval and inverse budget, and then comparing work among the candidates that meet the required recovery rate and forward fidelity. On the FFHQ64 panel in \cref{tab:recovery}, for example, an illustrative mean forward RMS ceiling of $0.03$ against Heun128 admits Heun2 among the maps with complete clean recovery at AA160. At a ceiling of $0.05$, DPM++2S also qualifies and uses fewer calls both to advance and to recover the state.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We asked when a diffusion sampling step keeps enough information to recover its input from a single saved output. For the coupled two-stage family with exact posterior means, the answer is a sign test. Nonnegative prediction weights give a global smooth inverse for every dimension, compact prior and noise level, and a negative weight lets a fixed two-image prior send three inputs to one output. For Heun's method this becomes a noise ratio of at least $1/2$ per substep and a minimum of $\lceil\log_2(a/b)\rceil$ substeps, while a single DPM-Solver++(2S) step always passes. On three pretrained models, the guaranteed DPM++2S step recovers every source under a shared budget, and one Heun step on the same interval fails on most sources under every optimizer and ceiling we tried. Among the steps that succeed, the choice is a measurable trade between inverse work and forward fidelity.

The study also has clear limits. The guarantee is universal over priors and scales and does not predict failure for a particular network: Heun2 lies outside it and still inverts well on our checkpoints, and mild negative weights in the controlled sweep keep full recovery and lower the forward error. Our learned-model evidence shows failed recovery and strong finite compression, but it does not exhibit an exact collision of a trained network, which so far is witnessed only analytically. The experiments use images of at most $64\times64$ pixels, one stressed interval, unconditional models and 32 to 64 sources per cohort, and the transfer condition \cref{eq:transfer} is not measured for any network.

These limits point to concrete next steps. Estimating $\operatorname{Lip}(\widehat F-F)$ or local co-Lipschitz constants for trained denoisers would connect the exact guarantee to specific checkpoints. Searching for near-collisions of learned steps along the directions where recovery fails would test whether the fold mechanism is what defeats the inverse. The analysis extends naturally to multistep and higher-stage solvers, to guided and latent diffusion, and to editing pipelines that store intermediate checkpoints. Finally, the sign test suggests a design principle for new solvers: choose combination weights that remain nonnegative, so that accuracy is gained without giving up output-only recovery.
