\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}
Diffusion and score-based models generate samples by reversing a noising process~\cite{ho2020,songsde2021}, and deterministic samplers integrate the associated probability-flow ODE with first-order steps such as DDIM~\cite{songddim2021} or with two-stage and multistep solvers such as Heun's method and DPM-Solver++~\cite{karras2022,lu2022}. Inverting these samplers underlies real-image editing. DDIM inversion runs the deterministic sampler approximately backwards~\cite{songddim2021}, and null-text inversion corrects the resulting drift by optimizing the unconditional text embedding~\cite{mokady2023}. Hong et al.~\cite{hong2024} instead solve the implicit equation of the executed DPM-Solver discretization, and timestep rescheduling chooses the inversion grid to reduce an error surrogate at a fixed budget~\cite{sun2026}. These methods search for a preimage of the executed map and are effective when one exists and is well conditioned, but they do not decide whether the map can be inverted from its output at all. A second line of work changes the map. EDICT, BDIA and BELM couple or extend the recurrence so that it is algebraically reversible given two retained states~\cite{wallace2023,zhang2024,wang2024}. Rex wraps exponential integrators, including DPM++2S as a base scheme, in a reversible coupling~\cite{rex2026}, and near-reversible explicit methods trade a small round-trip error for stability under edits~\cite{barancikova2026}. These designs buy exact reversal with extra state, whereas we ask when the ordinary single-output step already has it.

The closest theoretical result is Verbii's analysis of the exact one-prediction DDIM step~\cite{verbii2026}, which writes its inverse as a strongly convex program with a unique solution and shows that an amplitude-scaled, off-Bayes score can create multiple roots for a two-atom law. Our sufficient condition rests on the same structural facts, that a Gaussian posterior mean is the gradient of a convex function with covariance Jacobian~\cite{gribonval2021} and that strongly convex gradients are invertible~\cite{huang2021}, applied in the coordinates of the first stage. The converse is new to the coupled setting. It shows that exact Bayes predictions can themselves collide once one prediction is evaluated at a state produced by the other, and it turns into an exact schedule threshold for Heun. Requiring nonnegative combination weights echoes strong-stability-preserving Runge--Kutta methods, which are built from convex combinations of forward Euler steps~\cite{gottlieb2001,gottlieb2018}, although the property preserved here is injectivity rather than a norm bound. Supplement~I compares the reversal contracts of these approaches side by side.
