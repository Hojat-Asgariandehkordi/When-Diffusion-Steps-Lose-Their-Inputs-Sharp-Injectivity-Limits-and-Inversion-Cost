\section{Recovery, forward fidelity and work}
\label{sec:results}
We organize the results around three questions. Does a step outside the guarantee fail to recover sources in practice, and does a guaranteed step succeed? When several steps succeed, what distinguishes them? And is the failure caused by the sign of the first weight, or by other differences between the maps?

\emph{The guaranteed step recovers every source.} On the separately frozen FFHQ64 panel, DPM++2S and Heun2 each recover \FFHQDPMRecovered{} clean sources at the 160-forward ceiling (\cref{fig:tradeoff,tab:recovery}), and both also recover every noisy target at coordinate noise $10^{-3}$. One Heun step, whose first weight is $-6.5625$, recovers only \FFHQHeunOneRecovered{} clean sources. The paired difference in clean failure rate, DPM++2S minus Heun1, is \FFHQContrast{} percentage points with a 95\% seed-bootstrap interval. The same ordering holds on the two CIFAR-10 cohorts. DPM++2S recovers \PrimaryDPMRecovered{} clean and all noisy sources on the reused CIFAR-VP panel at \PrimaryCap{} forwards and \VEReconstruction{} on the separately frozen CIFAR-VE panel, while Heun1 recovers 18/64 and 10/32. Zero observed failures give two-sided exact 95\% upper failure limits of \PrimaryFailureBound{} at $N=64$ and \VEFailureBound{} at $N=32$.

\emph{Among successful steps, work and fidelity trade off.} \Cref{fig:tradeoff} places each map by its mean inverse work and its mean forward RMS, on a log axis so that the bootstrap intervals are visible; on FFHQ64 they are narrower than the markers, and dashed lines mark each cohort's AA ceiling.
DPM++2S needs two denoiser calls to advance the state and \FFHQDPMWork{} mean iterative calls to recover it. Heun2 needs four calls to advance and \FFHQHeunTwoWork{} to recover, but follows the fine trajectory more closely, with mean forward RMS \FFHQHeunTwoFlow{} against \FFHQDPMFlow{}. The arrow in \cref{fig:tradeoff} shows this exchange: the two maps reach the same recovery, and moving from DPM++2S to Heun2 halves the forward error at roughly double the inverse work. Heun2 succeeds even though both of its substep ratios are below $1/2$. The theorem guarantees invertibility for every prior; it does not predict failure for a particular learned network, and Heun2 is an example of a schedule outside the guarantee that inverts well on these checkpoints. On the reused CIFAR-VP panel, Euler2 is also successful with work similar to DPM++2S, sigma-midpoint has lower forward RMS but more recovery failures, and Heun2 again recovers all sources with the lowest forward error. On CIFAR-VE, Heun2 recovers 31/32. The Heun64/128 mean RMS disagreements of \FFHQRefinement{} on FFHQ64, \PrimaryRefinement{} on CIFAR-VP and \VERefinement{} on CIFAR-VE show that the Heun128 reference is converged far below these forward errors.

\emph{The first weight, with everything else fixed.} Heun1 and DPM++2S differ in more than the sign of $w_1$. Heun1 retains less of the input in its stage ($q=1/16$ against $1/4$) and evaluates its second prediction at a lower noise ($0.125$ against $0.5$). To isolate the weight, the fixed-stage sweep in \cref{fig:controlled} keeps the DPM++2S stage, $q=\kappa=1/4$, and varies only $w_1$ with $w_2=15/16-w_1$. Varying the weight moves the output along a single direction,
\begin{equation}
 F_{w_1}(x)=F_0(x)+w_1\bigl[D_a(x)-D_{a/4}(p_a(x))\bigr],
 \label{eq:sweep}
\end{equation}
so each arm is DPM++2S plus a multiple of the difference between the two predictions. On both intervals, $2\to1/8$ and $1\to1/16$, every weight between $-0.5$ and $0.5$ recovers all twelve sources, while transferring Heun1's weight $w_1=-6.5625$ to the DPM++2S stage reaches the ceiling on every source and recovers none (\cref{fig:controlled}a,b; dashed lines mark the recovery threshold and the ceiling). Mild negative weights are not harmful here. At $w_1=-0.5$ on $2\to1/8$, mean forward RMS drops from $0.0460$ to $0.0347$ relative to $w_1=0$, at the cost of $14.8$ more mean iterative calls (\cref{fig:controlled}c), and the lower-noise block shows the same exchange. Panel (c) shows mean forward RMS against Heun128, and panel (d) the seed median of the smallest centered finite-difference gain $g_{\rm rand}=\rms(F(x+hv)-F(x-hv))/(2h)$ over four random unit-RMS directions $v$ with $h=10^{-3}$. It is larger at $w_1=-6.5625$ than at zero in both blocks. Random directions therefore do not detect the sensitivity that defeats the inverse. These gains sample directional sensitivity at the source and are not singular values, so they do not locate it. The full table and paired contrasts are in Supplement~G.

\emph{Robustness to the optimizer, budget and interval.} The separation is a property of the step, not of the solver. Continuing from the Anderson iterate with warm-started L-BFGS or inexact Newton--GMRES, and charging every forward and backward traversal against ceilings of 80, 160 and 320, leaves the ordering unchanged. DPM++2S and Euler2 recover every clean source under all three procedures and ceilings, Heun2 misses at most two, and Heun1 still fails on about three quarters of the sources (Supplementary Fig.~1, which also shows the noisy arm). A contiguous 20\,s Anderson allocation including initialization agrees, and so does the native strong-Wolfe L-BFGS diagnostic, with \NativeHeunFailure{} clean Heun1 failures on the first eight reused indices at a mean of \NativeHeunWork{} charged traversals under an unequal-work protocol. The schedule axis behaves as the theory predicts. The geometric Heun4 schedule that meets the half-ratio criterion recovers \HeunFourRecovered{} clean sources at AA320 with CPU targets, with forward RMS \HeunFourFlow{} and \HeunFourWork{} mean iterative calls, and on the shorter $2\to1/2$ interval of an earlier GPU experiment all five maps recovered all 64 sources in both target arms under a ceiling of 80 map evaluations (with unequal denoiser work across maps). Supplements~E and F give the complete controls and their budgets.

\emph{Small residuals with large source errors.} A numerical inverse can stop at a point that fits the target well but is far from the source. On clean primary Heun1, \PrimaryHeunOneLowResidual{} misses at AA320 fall below the residual stop. For each such pair, with CPU map $F_{\mathrm{CPU}}$ and saved GPU target $y$, the triangle inequality bounds how far the CPU map separates the estimate from the true source,
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 &\rms\bigl(F_{\mathrm{CPU}}(\widehat x)-F_{\mathrm{CPU}}(x)\bigr)\\
 &\qquad\le e_y+\rms\bigl(y-F_{\mathrm{CPU}}(x)\bigr).
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:secant}
\end{equation}
Dividing this upper bound by $e_x$ gives a ratio of at most \PrimarySecantUpper{} on those pairs: the map compresses a source displacement of at least $0.02$ RMS to an output change more than four orders of magnitude smaller. Such finite-displacement compression is compatible with a poorly conditioned inverse. The CPU-target replay agrees on all \PrecisionFlags{} recovery flags at 160 forwards across the three checked maps, so the effect is not caused by the device change. These controls measure recovery and compression at finite precision. The exact multiplicity evidence remains the analytic witness in \cref{fig:collision}.
